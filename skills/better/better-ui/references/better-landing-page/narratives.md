# Narratives and site types

Choose the site type first, then the narrative that fits the visitor's intent. Both
are starting points; let the real content decide the number and order of sections.

## Site types

| Site | A successful visit ends in | First screen must show | Strongest proof | Usual sections |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product launch or SaaS | Trial, signup, demo, download | What it does, for whom, the product itself | The product working; named customers; specific results | Hero, product evidence, benefits, how it works, proof, pricing, FAQ, final action |
| Waitlist or pre-launch | Email captured | The promise and why now | The team, the problem's reality, early press | Hero with form, short story, what you get and when, form again |
| Studio, agency, freelancer | Inquiry or booking a call | Who they are, what they make, the work | Selected work with role and outcome; client names | Statement, work, services or approach, about, contact |
| Portfolio or personal site | Contact, follow, remembering the name | A person, their craft, a voice | The work itself; past teams; writing | One-sentence intro, work index, writing or side projects, contact |
| Small business (café, shop, salon, practice) | A visit, booking, call or order | What it is, where, the feel of the place | Photos of the real place and product; reviews; years open | Hero, offer or menu, the place, practical info, booking or directions |
| Event, conference, workshop | Ticket or registration | What, when, where, for whom | Speakers, past editions, attendee count, partners | Hero with date and action, lineup or program, venue, tickets, FAQ |
| Campaign or single offer | One action from one traffic source | The exact promise of the ad, email or post that sent them | One specific proof point | Short: hero, essential benefit, terms, action |
| Mission-led brand | Belief, then a product or newsletter step | A manifesto line over the real subject | Process, sourcing, press | Hero film or image, manifesto, process, products, press, newsletter |

A site can combine types: a product brand with a manifesto, a studio with a shop.
Keep one primary action per page and give the others secondary weight.

## Where the idea comes from

Fill the first blank of "On this page, [something of the subject] becomes [the
device]" from the client's own list, never from the category. A loaf of bread is
every bakery; the seven doors of the street this one is named after are only this
one. "Clean and modern" is not an idea.

| Look at | Ask | For example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Its output | What does it produce that can sit on the page as material? | A camera that prints poems delivers its copy as paper slips |
| Its object | What is its silhouette or cross-section? | The product's shape as the one motif, mask and frame |
| Its name | Can the letters hold a photograph, stack or split to two corners? | The name set full width with a picture inside it |
| Its place | Is there one view that makes someone want to be there? | One full-bleed view with the name along the bottom edge |
| Its ritual | Is there a sequence the page can perform as it scrolls? | The oven lit at five, the ferry at seven |
| Its artifact | What printed thing already exists in its world? | The page as the ticket, the menu, the label or the invitation |
| Its opposite | Which other category owns the feeling this one lacks? | A money app dressed as a streetwear drop |

## Narrative by visitor intent

| Visitor situation | Structure |
| --- | --- |
| Offer understood from a screenshot, photo or demo | Clear hero, evidence, benefits, objections, action |
| Unfamiliar offer or substantial skepticism | Explanatory story with proof beside each major claim; FAQ earlier |
| Known audience, simple offer, high intent (email to customers, event regulars) | Short page: essential benefit, terms, action |
| Comparing alternatives ("X vs Y", "best for") | Explicit criteria, supported differences, honest tradeoffs, next step |
| Browsing to judge taste or skill (portfolio, studio) | Identity line, then the work immediately; explanation on demand |
| Local and practical (where, when, how much) | Answer the practical questions in the first two screens; mood second |
| Belief-led (mission, craft, sustainability) | Manifesto told in scroll-sized beats, then the product as the payoff |

## Section purposes

Every section earns its place by doing one job. Use only those the argument needs.

| Section | Job | Keep it honest by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem to solution | Make the visitor recognize their situation | Using their words, not a straw man |
| Benefits (three to five) | Turn features into outcomes | Pairing each outcome with its mechanism or proof |
| How it works (about three steps) | Remove fear of effort | Showing real steps, real screens or real hands |
| Product or work evidence | Demonstrate instead of claim | Showing it legibly, at a meaningful crop |
| Statement or manifesto | Create a pause and a memorable line | Saying something specific to this brand |
| Proof (testimonials, logos, press, numbers) | Transfer trust | Attribution, context and verifiable sources |
| Comparison | Help a decision between options | Supportable criteria and admitted tradeoffs |
| Pricing or terms | Remove the cost question | Showing the real numbers and what is included |
| Practical info (hours, address, dates, venue) | Make the visit possible | Keeping it current and easy to scan |
| FAQ (as many as real questions) | Resolve the remaining objections | Answering directly; moving it earlier for high-friction offers |
| Risk reversal | Lower the cost of trying | Offering only real trials, refunds or cancellation |
| Final action | Close the page | The same action and label as the top |

## Primary actions by type

| Site | Primary action | Secondary, lower emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product | "Start free trial", "Get the app" | "Watch the demo", "See pricing" |
| Waitlist | "Join the waitlist" | "Read the story" |
| Studio or freelancer | "Start a project", "Book a call", an email address | "See all work" |
| Portfolio | An email or contact line | Social links, résumé |
| Small business | "Book a table", "Order", "Get directions" | "See the menu", "Opening hours" |
| Event | "Get tickets", "Register" | "See the program", "Become a partner" |
| Mission brand | "Find our foods", "Join the newsletter" | "Our process", "Watch the film" |

Name the outcome of the click. "Learn more", "Submit" and "Click here" say nothing.

## Outline before building

Before writing code for a new page, return in order:

1. Site type, narrative choice and why.
2. The idea sentence, and the live thing if there is one.
3. Section outline: one claim and its proof per section, with what is missing.
4. Hero copy: headline, supporting line, primary action, proof signal.
5. Indexing intent: evergreen and indexed, or a campaign kept out of search.

Then build the hero, establish the visual language, and work downward one section
at a time. Revise a settled section only when a later one changes the argument.
